# UX/UI audit: Two Forecasts, One Sky prototype

5 November 2026, against David's UX/UI standard (`ai-literacy-module/docs/ux-foundations.md`
and its concise twin): Nielsen's five usability goals, David's eight design principles,
WCAG 2.2 AA by POUR, cognitive accessibility (W3C, 2021), Mayer's signaling and cognitive
load, mindful UX, privacy by design, and the nine human–AI interaction themes.

**Method.** Automated checks in the browser (every text element's contrast against its
real background in both themes, headings, landmarks, control names, target sizes, line
length, reading level, third-party requests, storage), a keyboard pass, measurements at
375px and 1280px, and a review screen by screen. Not done: a test with real users, a
screen-reader pass (VoiceOver), and a check by someone color-blind. Those come next,
before anything is published.

## What already works

- **Contrast:** all 297 text elements pass in both themes: lowest 6.24:1 light, 7.62:1 dark
  (need 4.5:1). Edges and focus rings are above 3:1 (`scripts/palette.py`).
- **Reflow and resize:** no sideways scrolling at 320px, at 100% or 200% text, or at
  1280px with 200% text. Tables become stacked lists on narrow screens.
- **Targets and names:** every control is at least 44px tall and has a name.
- **Structure:** one h1, no skipped heading levels, header/main/footer landmarks, `lang`.
- **Keyboard:** every group is reachable, with a visible 3px amber focus ring.
- **Reading level:** about grade 5 (Flesch–Kincaid), longest sentence 25 words (limit 35).
- **Privacy:** no requests to other sites; the only storage is the viewer's theme choice.
- **Mindful UX:** nothing moves on its own; no streaks or scores; the guess feedback
  never judges the person; the scenario never scores anyone.
- **Graceful failure:** a missed station reading is named plainly, not hidden.
- **Calibrated trust:** the page says three evenings are too few to decide.

## Findings, most important first

Severity: **High** blocks or misleads; **Medium** slows or confuses many people;
**Low** polish. "Who" is who it affects.

| # | Finding | Principle / rule | Severity | Who | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Results are off-screen while choosing (phones).** At 375px the scenario's results start 1,519px (about two screens) below the first question. Changing an answer shows no visible change, so the live update is invisible. | Context (every action shows a result); Nielsen: visibility of system status | **High** | Most phone users of step 04 | A compact live summary that stays in view while answering (e.g. a sticky line: "In time 2 · False alarm 1 · Caught off guard 1"), linked to the full results; on wide screens, results beside the questions. |
| 2 | **Forecasts look more exact than they are.** Values like "18.1 °C" read as precise facts. Nothing says a forecast is an estimate with a range. | Human–AI: probabilistic foundation, calibrated trust | **Medium** | Everyone, most of all newcomers to forecasts | Show whole degrees in the drawing and table, with a one-line note: "Forecasts are estimates. A model can be off by a few degrees." Later: ranges from the ensembles (AIGEFS). |
| 3 | **Help is far from the words.** "AI model (AIGFS)", "GFS", "bias", "false alarm" are explained only in the glossary, about 12½ phone screens below the first use. | Cognitive accessibility: help one step away; nothing relies on memory | **Medium** | Readers new to weather or AI, people with cognitive disabilities | Explain each term where it first appears (a short plain gloss), and link the term to its glossary entry. |
| 4 | **"00:00 UTC, 4 November" under "3 November".** The evening labels carry a UTC time and a different date. That's jargon, and the date mismatch looks like an error. | User-centricity (the user's words); plain language | **Medium** | Most readers | Label "Evening of 3 November" with the local time in words ("about 7 p.m. in New York"); move UTC to the sources line. |
| 5 | **Celsius first for six US places.** Most US readers think in °F; every value makes them convert. | User-centricity; efficiency | **Medium** | US readers (the likely audience) | A °F / °C switch, remembered for the viewer (browser storage only), defaulting to °F for this US data. |
| 6 | **Lines are too long.** Running text reaches about 90–95 characters per line on wide screens (limit 75). `--measure: 62ch` is measured in zeros, which are wider than average letters in Atkinson. | Readability (line length 45–75); hierarchy | **Medium** | All readers on laptops and desktops | Set the measure in rem (about 34rem) and re-check by counting real characters per line. |
| 7 | **What the AI model can't do isn't said.** The page names the AI model but not its limits: it works on a grid about 25 km wide (it can't see a city park), and it doesn't forecast gusts, visibility or radar. | Human–AI: expectation setting | **Medium** | Everyone forming a view of AI forecasts | One line near the top or a finding card: "What the AI model can't do (yet)", with NOAA's own notice as the source. |
| 8 | **Step numbers jump.** Before a guess, the page shows 01, 02, "All cases", 04: step 03 is hidden until the result is shown. | Consistency; memorability | Low | Everyone, briefly | Keep the 03 band visible with a placeholder ("Make a guess, then show what happened"). |
| 9 | **The "Why" answer is chosen for you.** Step 04 pre-selects "Being caught off guard", a values question. A default can anchor the answer. | Wellbeing (neutral defaults); user control | Low | Everyone using step 04 | Leave "Why" unselected; the line under the results asks the person to choose. Other answers can keep defaults so results show at once. |
| 10 | **No skip link.** Keyboard users tab through the header first. Landmarks and headings meet WCAG 2.4.1, but the course standard includes a skip link. | Accessibility (operable); consistency with the course | Low | Keyboard and screen-reader users | "Skip to the activity" as the first link, visible on focus. |
| 11 | **The reveal button doesn't announce its state.** "Show / Hide what happened" changes its words, but has no `aria-expanded` or `aria-controls`. | WCAG 4.1.2 (name, role, value), robust | Low | Screen-reader users | Add `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls="result"`. |
| 12 | **Sources aren't one click away.** The footer and finding cards name NOAA's sources but don't link to them. | Human–AI: transparency | Low | Curious and skeptical readers | Link to NWS Service Change Notice 26-68, NOMADS and the NWS API (links load nothing until clicked). |
| 13 | **The theme button names the other theme.** "Light theme" on a dark page can read as the current state. | Consistency; user-centricity | Low | Some readers | "Switch to light theme", with `aria-pressed` or the course's existing pattern. |
| 14 | **No print view.** A teacher printing the page gets the dark theme and all the controls. | Context (classroom use) | Low | Teachers | A print style: light, on white paper, controls hidden, results shown. |

## Usability goals (Nielsen, 1993), in short

- **Learnability:** good. Guess first, then see, is clear. Findings 3 and 4 slow newcomers.
- **Efficiency:** good on desktop. On phones, finding 1 adds scrolling to every scenario change.
- **Memorability:** good. Numbered steps help; finding 8 breaks the sequence once.
- **Errors:** good. Guessing first is prompted, not forced, and every state can be undone ("Start again", any answer can change).
- **Satisfaction:** good. Calm and spacious, nothing nags. Not yet tested with people.

## Readability and flow audit (6 November 2026)

David asked: the font size in the buttons, and everything a person needs to use
the page with ease and flow: navigation, enjoyment, and more.

**Measured first.** Every text size in use: 13.6px (7 short labels), 15.3px
(43: small notes and sources), 17px (169: body text, every choice and button),
19.1px and up (headings, instructions, table numbers). Nothing under 13.6px
(floor 11px). Choices and buttons are 17px, Apple's size for body and button
text. Text spacing (WCAG 1.4.12: line height 1.5, letter 0.12em, word 0.16em,
paragraph 2em) caused no clipping and no sideways scrolling.

| # | Finding | Who | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **No way around a long page.** 12.7 phone screens with no page list, no "next" and no "back to top" (the course has all three). | Phone users; anyone returning to one part | "On this page" list under the opening notice; "Next" links after steps 01, 03 and the bigger picture; "Back to top" at the end |
| R2 | **The first choice was 1.27 screens down on a phone**; the opening alone was 489px. | Phone users | Shorter opening on phones (404px) and less space above the first section. The page list adds a little height, so step 01 is now 1.5 screens down, but its link is on the first screen |
| R3 | **"Make a guess first" wasn't announced.** Focus moved, but the reason was silent. | Screen-reader users | The hint is a status message (`role="status"`) |
| R4 | **"Colder than 50 °F 10 °C"** read as one run-on phrase. | Screen-reader users; everyone, a little | Secondary units in brackets: "Colder than 50 °F (10 °C)" |
| R5 | **Labels in capitals** ("BEFORE YOU START", "ALL CASES"…). Capitals are slower to read and harder for many dyslexic readers. | Dyslexic readers; everyone, a little | Sentence case, 15.3px, semibold |
| R6 | **Header buttons at 15.3px**, while every other button is 17px. | Everyone (consistency) | 17px |
| R7 | **Line height 1.55**, below the course's 1.62. | Readers of longer text | 1.6 |
| R8 | **No next move after a result.** The result ended, and the person had to find their own way on. | Everyone (flow) | "Try another case ↑" and "Next: the bigger picture ↓" after every result |
| R9 | **Findings told, but didn't invite.** The Central Park finding said the AI model ran cold, with no way to see it. | Everyone (discovery, enjoyment) | A "Try it: New York, 4 November" button loads that case, moves to the guess and announces it |
| R10 | **No sense of how long it takes, and no ending.** | Everyone (expectation, mindful pause) | "It takes about 10 minutes" at the top; a calm ending: "That's the whole activity. Take a break, or come back and try another place." |
| R11 | **Two hairlines stacked under the header.** | Everyone (visual noise) | The first section drops its own line |

**Re-checked:** all 340 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 5.5:1
light, 7.24:1 dark); every target at least 44px; no skipped headings; no
sideways scrolling at 375px with 200% text; every page-list link lands on its
section.

**For David to decide (not changed):** the page uses British spelling
("organizer", "center", "color") while the data and likely audience are in the
US. US spelling would read more naturally to US learners; the course uses
British spelling.

## Map, search and assistive-technology checks (6 November 2026)

- **Map:** the 48 contiguous states in one Albers equal-area projection, so the scale bar
  (500 km, 300 mi) is honest near the middle. Each station's shape says which model did
  better there (circle, square, diamond), so color is never the only cue. On narrow
  maps the name tags give way to a place list, so tags never crowd; the list is the
  keyboard and screen-reader path, and the pins are a pointer shortcut. At 320px two pins
  touch, which WCAG 2.5.8 allows because the list offers the same choice at full size.
- **Search:** a labeled field; every word must match; titles rank higher; results are
  buttons that jump to a case, a place, a word, a finding or a scenario role; the count
  and "no results" are announced; Escape clears; what's typed is escaped, never markup.
- **Screen-reader structure:** no duplicate IDs; every input labeled; landmarks: banner,
  "On this page" navigation, search, main, footer; four live regions; five tables with
  captions and headers; no unlabeled drawings; nothing focusable inside hidden content.
- **Forced colors (Windows high contrast):** selection and focus shown with system
  colors; the map and buttons keep their edges. Not yet seen on Windows.
- **Re-check:** 422 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 5.5:1); every
  target 44px or more; no skipped headings; no requests to other sites; no sideways
  scrolling at 320px or 375px, at 100% or 200% text.
