# UX/UI audit: making it professional (19 November 2026)

The two earlier audits (`ux-audit-2026-11-05.md`) made the page work: accessible, readable
and easy to move around. This one asks what makes it read as a finished, trustworthy product
for its audiences (educators, professionals, students, forecasters and emergency managers),
now that it is public.

Measured on the live page (https://davidpetry-cloud.github.io/two-forecasts-one-sky/): 10
requests, about 220 KB transferred (455 KB unpacked), loaded in under a second; no requests to
other sites; one h1, no skipped headings. VoiceOver and a session with real people were done
by David on 19 November and reported good.

## Professional upgrades, ranked

**Tier 1: credibility and finish** (small changes, large effect on trust)

| # | Upgrade | Why (principle, goal or theme) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **An "About and methods" section**: what the data is, how cases are compared, the rain rules, what the AI model can't do, how often it updates, with links to the protocol, the roadmap and the code on GitHub | Human–AI: *transparency* and *expectation setting*; principle: *context*; goal: *learnability*. Professionals and educators look for methods before they trust a number. |
| P2 | **Numbers with their sample size, and "too close to call"**: every comparison shows its count and share ("31 of 96, 32%"), and differences smaller than the day-to-day noise say so instead of naming a winner | Human–AI: *calibrated trust* and *probabilistic foundation*. Today a 0.1 °F difference reads as a win. |
| P3 | **Open data you can actually take**: "Download the data (JSON, CSV)" links, with the CC0 note, and a "How to cite" line (plus a `CITATION.cff` file) | Principle: *user-centricity* (researchers, teachers); the open-data promise made usable. |
| P4 | **Link previews and a tab icon**: a meta description, Open Graph and social card with a designed preview image, an SVG favicon and touch icon, and a browser theme color for each theme | Principle: *consistency* (the product looks the same everywhere it appears). A shared link currently shows a blank card. |
| P5 | **Statements people expect**: a short accessibility statement (WCAG 2.2 AA target, how it was tested, known limits, how to report a problem) and a privacy note ("no cookies or tracking; your theme and unit choices stay in your browser") | Principles: *accessibility* and *wellbeing* (privacy by design: say what happens to data). |
| P6 | **A way to give feedback**: "Report a problem or suggest an idea" through GitHub issue forms (no email exposed) | Goals: *errors* and *satisfaction*; principle: *user control*. |
| P7 | **A version and "what's new"**: a small "Version 0.4 · What's new" link to a short changelog, beside "Saved through the evening of …" | Human–AI: *sustained reliance* (changes announced); goal: *memorability*. |

## The standard, item by item

**Nielsen's usability goals (Nielsen, 1993)**

| Goal | Where it stands | To lift it |
|---|---|---|
| Learnability | Good: guess first, then see; help next to the words; tested with people | P1, P11 |
| Efficiency | Good on desktop; phones have a page list and next links | P8, P12 |
| Memorability | Good: numbered steps | P7 |
| Errors | Good: nothing to lose; every choice can change | P6 |
| Satisfaction | Good: calm, spacious; session reported good | P9, P11 |

**David's eight design principles** (each rests on its own foundation; see the standard's
"Where these come from")

| Principle | Where it stands | To lift it |
|---|---|---|
| User-centricity | US spelling, °F first, plain words | P3, P14 |
| Consistency | One type scale, one button ring, one name per action | P4 |
| Hierarchy | Numbered bands, hairlines, alternating shades | P8 |
| Context | Every action shows a result; live summary in the scenario | P1, P10 |
| User control | Start again, any answer can change, °F/°C and theme switches | P6 |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA checks pass; VoiceOver good; forced-colors rules | P5, P13 |
| Usability | Session reported good | P9 |
| Wellbeing | No streaks, scores or autoplay; nothing stored or sent | P5, P11 |

**Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA, by POUR) and cognitive accessibility (W3C, 2021):** all
automated checks pass in both themes (contrast, 44px targets, reflow at 320px, 200% text,
text spacing, headings, landmarks, names, live regions). Still open: the Windows
forced-colors look. Cognitive accessibility: plain words (about grade 5), one instruction
per step, help beside the words.

**Signaling and cognitive load (Mayer, 2021; Sweller, 1988):** strong signaling (bands,
numbers, hairlines); segmenting by step. P9 would add the missing visual cue for patterns.

**Privacy by design (Cavoukian, 2009):** private by default, nothing collected, no
third-party requests. Missing: saying so on the page (P5).

**Human–AI interaction** (themes credited to uxprinciples.design, anchored in Amershi et
al., 2019; Horvitz, 1999; Lee and See, 2004; Google PAIR; Shneiderman, 2022)

| Theme | Where it stands | To lift it |
|---|---|---|
| Probabilistic foundation | Whole degrees; "forecasts are estimates" | P2 |
| Expectation setting | "What the AI model can't do yet" | P1 |
| Calibrated trust | Findings follow the data and say when evenings are too few | P2, P10 |
| Transparency | Sources in the footer and finding cards | P1, P3 |
| Control and agency | The learner chooses every case and rule | (met) |
| Graceful failure | Missed readings named; archive heals itself | P13 |
| Co-creation | The scenario is the learner's own rule | (met) |
| Responsible autonomy | No AI acts on the learner's behalf | (met) |
| Sustained reliance | "Saved through …", nightly updates | P7 |

**Mindful UX:** calm defaults, natural pause points (the closing line), no addictive
patterns. P11 adds a reflective ending.

## Recommendation

Do Tier 1 next (P1 to P7): a day's work, mostly words and metadata, and the biggest step in
how professional the page feels. Then P10 (rain on the page), since its data is already
being saved, and P8 and P9 together, which change the first impression and how the patterns
are seen.

## Tier 1 built (19 November 2026)

All seven, plus P13 (a message if scripts are off):
- **P1, P5:** an "About and methods" section (the data, how a case is compared, how a winner
  is called, rain, limits, updates, accessibility, privacy, sources, how to cite), linked from
  the page list, the footer and search.
- **P2:** a model is called better only when its average advantage is more than twice its
  standard error; otherwise "too close to call". Every count shows its share.
- **P3:** `site/data/cases.csv` and `cases.json` (CC0), rebuilt with the page; `CITATION.cff`.
- **P4:** description, canonical link, Open Graph and social card with a 1200×630 preview
  image, SVG favicon, touch icon, theme color.
- **P6:** GitHub issue forms, "Report a problem" and "Suggest an idea", warning against
  personal details.
- **P7:** version 0.4 in the footer and the citation; `CHANGELOG.md`.

Checked: 705 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 6.0:1); no skipped headings;
no sideways scrolling at 320px, at 100% or 200% text; About lines at most 70 characters;
downloads, icons and preview image all serve.

## Tier 2, P10 built: rain on the page (19 November 2026, version 0.5)

- **Each case** gets "And rain?": what each model said for the 6 hours before the evening, what
  the station measured, and whether each was right. No guess (coherence: step 02 stays about
  temperature). Missing rain is named and explained, never shown as dry.
- **The bigger picture** gets a Rain table (caught the rain, missed it, false alarm, right that
  it stayed dry), a stacked twin for phones, and a finding card with a "Try it" case.
- **Calibrated trust:** the same twice-the-standard-error rule as for temperature, plus a floor
  of 10 rainy place-evenings, because one rainy evening counts up to three times. At build:
  72 cases from 6 evenings, rain in 12, from 7 rainy place-evenings, so "too early to call",
  though the early sign favors the AI model (caught 12 of 12 against 7 of 12).
- **The scenario** gets "Rain" as a "What" choice, with an action for each role; cases with no
  rain record are counted out and the reason is given.
- **Units:** rain follows the switch (inches with °F, millimeters with °C).

David also asked (19 November) for **icons for easier navigation**: one set of line icons,
drawn once and reused, beside every "On this page" entry, each section's number and the rain
headings, always next to words (never an icon alone), in the text color, so they work in both
themes and in forced colors. A Rain entry joined the page list.

Checked: 698 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 5.5:1); one h1, no skipped
headings, no duplicate ids; no sideways scrolling at 320px at 100% or 200% text; page-list
targets 44px.

## Tier 2, P8 and P9 built (19 November 2026, version 0.6)

- **P8, a clear start:** one primary action in the opening ("Start with a case ↓", 48px), a note
  on time and no sign-up, and a strip of what's in the record (evenings saved, cases checked,
  weather stations, latest evening). One change from the plan: the strip doesn't name who's
  ahead, because that would answer step 02's guess before it's asked (learning design:
  predict, then see).
- **P9, charts:** a dot plot of average miss by forecast length (the gap drawn between the
  models) and a line chart of each evening's average miss, which shows the day-to-day swings
  behind "too close to call". Both are drawn at the box's real width (text 14px or more), have
  a spoken summary, a legend and a table twin; the evening chart has a crosshair readout for the
  pointer and the arrow keys, announced in the live region. Identity is shape and color.
- **Chart colors:** the model colors used as text (`--ai`, `--phys`) failed the chart
  validator's lightness and chroma bands as marks, so marks now use `--ai-mark` and
  `--phys-mark` (same hues): colorblind separation ΔE 20 or more, contrast 3:1 or more on
  every surface, in both themes (also in `scripts/palette.py`).
- **Prototype** (David, 19 November): the name carries "prototype" until the design is set.

Checked: 850 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 6.0:1), chart marks 4.25:1 or
more; one h1, no skipped headings or duplicate ids; no sideways scrolling at 320px at 100% or
200% text, with the chart readout open; chart text 14px.

## Tier 2, P11 and P12 built (19 November 2026, version 0.7): Tier 2 complete

- **P11, learning bookends:** "What you'll be able to do" at the top, three observable outcomes
  (compare, explain, choose; backward design). At the end, a "Before you go" band: one
  reflection question, ideas one step away in a disclosure (cognitive accessibility: help
  nearby), nothing to type or save (privacy, mindful UX: a natural pause point), and a look
  back at the three outcomes with links to try again. Added to the page list and search.
- **P12, a lighter map:** the land is filled from the outline instead of drawing every state
  twice, lines are simplified (topojson-simplify, 1e-3 square degrees) and rounded to whole map
  units: 198 KB to 37 KB, checked side by side at the page's size. `map.js` now loads when the
  bigger picture comes within about a screen of view; its space is held, so nothing jumps; if it
  fails, the page says so and the list, panel and table carry the same information (graceful
  failure, the rest of P13). The address carries a version so browsers fetch the new file.

Checked: 874 text elements pass contrast in both themes (lowest 6.0:1); one h1, no skipped
headings, no duplicate ids, no broken in-page links; disclosures 44px; no sideways scrolling
at 320px at 100% or 200% text (a long word in the reflection question wrapped to fix it).

## Tier 3, P15 built, and a print bug fixed (19 November 2026, version 0.8)

- **P15, an educator pack:** `site/educators.html`, a lesson plan in this project (not the
  course; whether it joins the Human-Centered AI Literacy course is David's decision). At a
  glance, the same three outcomes, before class, five timed steps (question, one case together,
  pairs, roles in the scenario, close with the reflection), what to listen for (four common
  ideas, each with a question), discussion questions, access and care (including a gentler
  role for someone the weather frightens). Print: two and a half pages at 10pt, with link
  addresses written out. The classroom version of the scenario is the scenario itself, run by
  roles; no separate build.
- **Print bug (all pages):** the print rules lost to the dark-mode color rules, so a dark-mode
  device printed near-white text; and the alternate bands' full-width shadow, white on paper,
  spilled across page breaks and hid the next section. Fixed: print colors match every theme,
  and the shading is dropped on paper. Printing now also fetches the map first.
- **Windows high contrast, simulated:** Chrome's forced-colors emulation over the DevTools
  protocol, dark and light: text, links, buttons, the units switch, chosen answers, tables, the
  charts (shapes kept) and the map all hold. The page's accessibility statement says it was a
  simulation; a real Windows check remains with David.

Checked: the plan page passes contrast in both themes (99 text elements, lowest 6.0:1), one h1,
no skipped headings, 44px targets, no sideways scrolling at 320px at 100% or 200% text; it
prints complete from light and dark devices.
