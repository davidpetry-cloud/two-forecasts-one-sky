# User session plan: Two Forecasts, One Sky

A short, kind session with three to five people, to see whether the page works for
real learners. Prepared 6 November 2026 for David to run. Five people find most of the
problems a page has (Nielsen); three already find many.

## Who to invite

Aim for a spread, not a sample:

| Who | Why |
|---|---|
| An educator | The course's first audience; will they teach with it? |
| A professional outside weather | Can a smart non-expert follow it? |
| A student, 18 or over | The students' track, without the consent steps a minor needs |
| A forecaster or emergency manager | Is it fair to the models and to their work? |
| Someone who uses a screen reader, magnification or high contrast | The real accessibility check, beyond the automated ones |

**Minors:** only through a school, with the school's and parents' consent and the
course's safeguarding route (`ai-literacy-module/docs/student-safety.md`). Not for a first
round.

## Privacy and consent (privacy by design)

- Say what the session is for, how long it takes (about 30 minutes), and that they can stop
  at any time without giving a reason.
- **Collect only notes.** No names in the notes (use P1, P2…), no recording unless they
  agree out loud, and then delete the recording once the notes are written.
- Nothing they type in the page is saved: it has no accounts and sends nothing anywhere.
  Say so.
- Thank them; don't score them. It's the page being tested, not the person.

## Setup

- The live page (https://davidpetry-cloud.github.io/two-forecasts-one-sky/) on their own device, or on David's Mac.
- Light or dark theme, °F or °C: their choice. Let them use their own assistive tech
  if they have it.
- Ask them to think aloud: "Say what you're looking for, what you expect, and what surprises you."

## Tasks (read each one aloud; don't help unless they're stuck for a minute)

1. **First look (1 minute).** "What is this page for? What would you do first?"
2. **Guess and see.** "Pick any place and evening, make a guess, and see what happened."
3. **Find a pattern.** "Which model ran cold at Central Park? Find where the page says so."
4. **Use the map.** "Which place did the physics model do better at? How did you find it?"
5. **Make a decision.** "You're a farmer. You'll cover the crops if it'll be colder than
   50 °F, deciding 3 days ahead. Which rule would you choose, and why?"
6. **Search.** "Find the word 'bias'." Then: "Find the New York case from 4 November."

## What to watch for (one line per task)

- Where they hesitate, scroll back, or look for something that isn't there.
- Words they don't understand, or read differently from what we meant.
- Whether they trust the AI model more or less by the end, and why.
- Anything that delights them, and anything that annoys them.
- For assistive-tech users: what's announced, what's missing, what's announced twice.

## Questions at the end

- "In one sentence, what did you learn?" (learning: Kirkpatrick level 2)
- "Would you use this, or share it? With whom?" (relevance)
- "What was the hardest part?" and "What was the best part?"
- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- Nielsen's goals in plain words, 1 to 5: easy to start? quick once you knew it? would you
  remember how? any mistakes? pleasant to use?

## After the sessions

- Gather the notes into one list: what happened, how many people it happened to, how bad it was.
- Fix in order of harm and reach; add each finding to `docs/ux-audit-2026-11-05.md`.
- Delete anything that could identify a person.
