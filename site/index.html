Two Forecasts, One Sky

NOAA now runs an AI weather model beside its physics model. Guess which one came closer, then see what really happened.

Before you start

This is a prototype for learning, not a forecast. For real forecasts and warnings, go to weather.gov. The AI model is new: see what it can't do yet. It takes about 10 minutes.

Try "Central Park", "4 November", "bias" or "farmer".

    Glossary

    Words used here

    Forecast
    An estimate of what the weather will do. It can be a few degrees off, so this page rounds forecasts to whole degrees.
    Model
    A computer program that works out what the weather will do next.
    AI model (AIGFS)
    NOAA's model that learned from years of past weather. It runs on a tiny fraction of the computing the physics model needs.
    Physics model (GFS)
    NOAA's model built from the laws of motion, heat and moisture.
    Miss
    How far a forecast was from what the thermometer measured.
    Running cold
    Forecasting lower temperatures than really happened, again and again. Scientists call a lean like this a bias.
    Rule
    What you decide to do with the forecasts, such as "act only if both models agree".
    False alarm
    You acted, and the weather didn't come.
    Caught off guard
    The weather came, and you hadn't acted.
